\phaseheader{4}{Construct intervention-specific recoverability surfaces}{Create the paper's central causal dataset by branching multiple recovery operators at multiple prespecified times from identical long-horizon states, then estimate where each intervention helps, harms, or becomes too late.}

\subsection{Scientific hypotheses}

This phase tests three hypotheses:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\Delta_k(h_t,d)$ depends on active stage, failure mode, and recovery operator;
  \item recoverability can be non-monotonic because of self-correction and stage transitions;
  \item terminal failure confidence is an imperfect ranking of intervention advantage and time remaining before the last useful intervention.
\end{enumerate}

The contribution is not the generic observation that long delays are bad. The distinctive object is a dense, paired, multi-operator surface measured from identical simulator-policy states. This differs from applying one repair at one trigger and sweeping only execution latency.

\paragraph{Operator interface.}
Define a common recovery operator API with explicit \code{prepare}, \code{act}, and \code{end} methods plus a cost record. Phase~4 must use only the deployable recovery mechanism or mechanisms that showed headroom in Phase~3B; it must not populate an expensive grid with failed arms. The available registry should include:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{continue}: no intervention;
  \item \code{history\_reset\_replan}: clear policy image/state context and replan; a cache-only flush is redundant at empty-cache inference boundaries;
  \item \code{stage\_retry}: re-prompt with the current atomic goal; the completed screen used supervisor-supplied goals, while a deployable causal stage tracker remains to be validated;
  \item \code{retreat\_reset\_replan}: execute a frozen bounded retreat/open-gripper/reset sequence, then replan;
  \item no candidate-selection arm under the current evidence; such an arm could return only after a materially different proposal family establishes new oracle headroom under a separate frozen screen;
  \item \code{env\_to\_last\_good}: optional simulator-oracle diagnostic requiring genuine earlier anchors; none were executed in the operator screen and its success is not a guaranteed upper bound.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{State and delay grid.}
Use three detector-independent intervention landmarks---early, middle, and late---within the active stage as the primary design. Generate all three from the same stage-entry snapshot: restore that state, follow the same frozen nominal trajectory to the assigned intervention time, apply the operator, and evaluate at a common absolute endpoint measured from stage entry. This identifies a dynamic intervention-regime contrast under shared pre-intervention history. Merely comparing unrelated early and late snapshots does not prove that delay caused the difference.

For trigger-conditioned latency analysis, apply delays such as $\mathcal{D}=\{0,1,2,4\}$ genuine inferences only on a smaller secondary subset. Keeping this factor out of the primary grid prevents a combinatorial expansion. Cached environment actions are not independent warning opportunities. Equal post-intervention suffix length may be reported as a secondary compute-matched view, but it must not replace the common-endpoint primary analysis because it grants later interventions more total task time.

\paragraph{Analysis tooling.}
Add a surface builder that estimates $R_k$, $\Delta_k$, uncertainty, task/stage support, censoring, and the operational deadline in Equation~\eqref{eq:deadline}. It must preserve the difference between natural and injected failures and between oracle and deployable context.

\subsection{Experimental design}

\textbf{Phase 4A: development, support, and variance.} Select eight composite tasks before inspecting surface outcomes, stratified by horizon, semantic-stage count, baseline success, and failure support. Reserve whole task or scene identities rather than descendants of development parents. A planning design is eight tasks, 12 parent identities per task, three common-base intervention times, four arms including \code{continue}, and two predeclared continuation-seed schedules, yielding $8\times12\times3\times4\times2=2{,}304$ suffix executions before censoring. Retain only operators supported by the higher-level operator screen and reduce the arm count rather than padding the study with failed mechanisms. On a stratified subset, use additional continuation seeds to estimate seed variance.

Open Phase~4A arm identities only for support, variance, operator, and threshold selection. Freeze the retained arms, endpoints, and allocation rule afterward. Expand to approximately 12 tasks only through a separate \textbf{Phase 4B} confirmation with sealed parents and whole task or scene identities. The same 2,304 branches cannot serve as both method-selection and confirmatory evidence.

For a single snapshot and a single seed, the outcome is a realized finite-seed utility, not a probability. Define the empirical operator value as
\begin{equation}
  \hat{R}_k(h_t,d)=\operatorname{mean}_{m=1,\ldots,M}Y_{k,m}(h_t,d),
\end{equation}
where the $M$ continuation seeds are declared before branching. Population-level recovery probabilities average these finite-seed outcomes across prespecified snapshots and tasks. Reports must show $M$, seed variance, and sensitivity to the continuation schedule rather than treating one suffix as an exact-state probability estimate.

Natural failures and successful controls are primary. Injected actuation noise, visual occlusion, distractors, or state perturbations may be added as a separate stress-test stratum. They must not be pooled into natural failure prevalence or the primary deployment estimand.

\subsection{Evaluation and scientific tests}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Recoverability curves.} Plot $R_k(h_t,d)$ and $\Delta_k(h_t,d)$ by task, stage, operator, and delay with task/parent/snapshot uncertainty.
  \item \textbf{Deadline.} Estimate the last supported delay at which the lower confidence bound of $\Delta_k$ exceeds a positive margin. Report grid-resolution uncertainty rather than pretending to know a continuous exact time.
  \item \textbf{Integrated value.} Summarize the area under the supported $\Delta_k$ curve, weighting tasks equally and reporting the supported risk set.
  \item \textbf{Heterogeneity.} Fit an interpretable mixed-effects or hierarchical model with task/stage/failure-type effects, and retain raw task-level curves.
  \item \textbf{Failure probability versus value.} Compare SAFE score ranking with realized $\Delta_k$, best-operator identity, and deadline. Use within-snapshot and task-level analyses.
  \item \textbf{Rescue and harm.} Report rescue among failed nominal suffixes and corruption among successful nominal suffixes. Do not summarize only average success.
  \item \textbf{Downstream retention.} Report whether an apparent rescue persists through the next stage landmark and to final task outcome when supported.
  \item \textbf{Risk sets.} Publish the number of states still eligible at every later landmark; stages that naturally terminate before the late intervention cannot silently disappear from denominators.
\end{enumerate}

\positiveresult{Phase~4A supports several task-stage strata in which at least one deployable operator has positive paired advantage, the best operator or deadline varies across states, and failure probability does not perfectly rank causal recovery value. Support is distributed across tasks rather than one favorable stage, immediate gains persist downstream, and the resulting variance estimate makes a sealed Phase~4B feasible. Only Phase~4B can confirm the frozen surface and justify the corresponding general claim.}

\negativeresult{If only environment rewind helps, the current deployable recovery operators are insufficient. If all deployable $\Delta_k$ values are near zero, narrow the project to a diagnostic benchmark or change the recovery mechanism. If only one task contributes signal, do not claim a general long-horizon recoverability surface.}

\subsection{Validity controls}

Branch-state selection must be detector-independent. Later prefixes require explicit risk-set denominators because short stages have already completed or failed. Common randomness must be shared until treatment divergence. Oracle simulator stage, oracle failure onset, and environment rewind must be shown separately from the deployable controller.

\subsection{Required outputs}

The dataset release should contain separate Phase~4A and Phase~4B manifests, full snapshot hashes, branch provenance, natural/injected labels, common-base and continuation-randomness schedules, common absolute endpoints, support/censoring and risk-set tables, recoverability curves, deadline estimates, downstream retention, rescue/harm plots, and a failure taxonomy with representative videos.
