\subsection{Scientific hypothesis}

For an identical observation and a frozen decision set, let $A^\star$ denote the learned or selected recovery rule and $A^c$ its strongest matched control. Then
\begin{equation}
  H_6:\quad
  \Delta_{\mathrm{decision}}
  = \Pr(Y_{\mathrm{stage}}=1\mid A^\star)
  -\Pr(Y_{\mathrm{stage}}=1\mid A^c)>0.
\end{equation}
The fixed trigger deliberately removes detector timing from this experiment. If the chosen mechanism fails here, a later end-to-end result cannot be repaired by claiming that the trigger was responsible.

\subsection{Pilot design}

Use the five development tasks and 10 new parent identities per task. Trigger recovery at a fixed detector-independent stage prefix or simulator-oracle snapshot shared across arms so that only the recovery decision changes. Freeze exactly one route:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{candidate route:} nominal $K=1$, random $K=4$, lowest terminal-SAFE $K=4$, and highest predicted recovery-value $K=4$;
  \item \textbf{operator route:} \code{continue}, the strongest fixed deployable operator, random eligible operator, and router-selected operator;
  \item \textbf{fixed-operator route:} \code{continue} versus the single supported fixed operator, with more identities allocated to the two-arm comparison rather than inventing selector ablations.
\end{itemize}
The four-arm routes produce 200 high-level arm rollouts. Compute finite-set oracle selection offline from the Phase~3B--5 corpus; do not execute it as a deployable arm.

Block and interleave assignments by task and server session. Match the initial environment seed/reset and ordinary-policy sampling family. Also match candidate seeds, recovery horizon, and the fallback seed schedule. Run arms sequentially against one stateful server or use separately validated servers with the same model and sampling behavior.

\subsection{Evaluation}

The primary endpoint is active-stage recovery. Full-task success, ordered progress, completed-stage regression, collision/safety events, number of policy calls, and wall-clock latency are secondary. Report:
\begin{itemize}
  \item learned selection minus the strongest matched random and fixed controls;
  \item terminal SAFE candidate selection when the candidate route is active;
  \item generic proposal or operator benefit relative to nominal continuation;
  \item regret to finite-set oracle selection;
  \item effects conditional on causal headroom, diversity, stage, and predicted uncertainty.
\end{itemize}

Use paired task/parent bootstrap or a blocked randomization test that preserves initial identity. Errors after assignment count against the assigned arm in the primary intention-to-treat analysis; a per-protocol sensitivity may be reported separately.

\positiveresult{This 50-identity pilot is a screening gate, not the definitive efficacy test. On a learned-selection route, proceed when the model improves task-macro active-stage recovery over the strongest fixed deployable control by a frozen practically meaningful margin, approximately 10 percentage points, without one-task dependence or material harm. On the fixed-operator route, require benefit over continuation with the same safety guardrails. Report the paired hierarchical interval and width, but do not require this small pilot to exclude zero; Phase~7 must power and confirm the frozen primary comparison.}

\negativeresult{Do not implement flow guidance. If offline selection passed but the prospective fixed-trigger test fails, preserve the discrepancy as distribution-shift or proxy-mismatch evidence. One new development cohort may be justified, but never tune on the prospective identities. If a fixed operator also fails, no learned trigger can make that executor useful.}
