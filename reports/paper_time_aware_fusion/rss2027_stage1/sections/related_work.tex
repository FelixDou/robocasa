\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Failure detection from policy internals.}
SAFE learns a failure score from a VLA's last-layer features and thresholds it with a
functional conformal band~\cite{gu2025safe}; we reuse its implementation. Follow-ups change
the training data~\cite{rajaprakash2026safecast}, labels~\cite{park2026hideseek,ma2026failurespot},
or policy~\cite{soddy2026memoryvla}. FIPER notes that accumulated scores flag failures mostly
because failed rollouts are longer~\cite{romer2025fiper}, and ActProbe feeds the normalized
timestep to its model~\cite{huang2026actprobe}, but none reports an elapsed-time detector. SAFE identifies the duration shortcut and removes it by
evaluating rollouts at equal length (its App.~B.5) and, on its real robot, by running every
rollout to a fixed horizon~\cite{gu2025safe}. These controls suit fixed-horizon evaluation;
under success-terminated execution, duration is a shortcut~\cite{geirhos2020shortcut} for offline
metrics but legitimate online evidence, much as time limits make the remaining time part of the
agent's state in reinforcement learning~\cite{pardo2018timelimits}.

\textbf{Action-space and success-only detectors.}
STAC~\cite{agia2024sentinel} and VLA-FAIL~\cite{seligmann2026vlafail} compare overlapping
action chunks, and ActProbe~\cite{huang2026actprobe} feeds chunk consistency, magnitude and the
normalized timestep to a task-conditioned LSTM, beating internal-feature baselines on RoboCasa. FAIL-Detect~\cite{xu2025faildetect} and
FIPER~\cite{romer2025fiper} score inputs, outputs or embeddings against successful rollouts
only. FIPER alarms when two conformal scores \emph{both} exceed their thresholds, which
suppresses false alarms; we alarm when \emph{either} crosses, as the two signals catch failures
at different times. CheckVLA~\cite{liu2026checkvla}, the closest work on our benchmark,
verifies each action chunk with a world model on RoboCasa365 at a conformal 5\% rate of
unnecessary interventions and repairs it with the same VLA. Neither FIPER nor CheckVLA compares
against elapsed time.

\textbf{Time, calibration and intervention.}
FIPER argues that late detection is not enough~\cite{romer2025fiper} and Hide-and-Seek weights
accuracy by time~\cite{park2026hideseek}. Our lead is one minus FIPER's detection time and, when
failures run to the limit, determines Hide-and-Seek's TWA at equal FPR (Sec.~\ref{sec:setup}): the
metric is not new, the duration-only comparator is. Lead at a fixed false-alarm budget is the
accuracy--earliness trade-off of early time-series classification~\cite{xing2012early}.
Split-conformal thresholds give finite-sample
false-positive control under exchangeability~\cite{angelopoulos2021gentle}; our two-signal OR
rule (Sec.~\ref{sec:guarantee}) is an instance of nested conformal
prediction~\cite{gupta2022nested}, and Learn-then-Test~\cite{angelopoulos2025ltt} could choose
its path from data with a guarantee. RESCUE~\cite{ahmedt2026rescue}
finds that good detection scores do not guarantee lower intervention cost, and CheckVLA
couples detection to repair; an earlier alarm matters only if acting
on it raises success.
