\section{Technical summary}

\subsection{Question}
Can SAFE-style detectors predict that a RoboCasa policy will fail early enough to support intervention, using internal action-policy features rather than only simulator state or elapsed time?

\subsection{Answer}
The integration and experimental protocol are valid, and RLDX-1 action features contain useful retrospective failure information. The strongest rollout-level RLDX MLP achieved task-normalized ROC-AUC \metric{0.713 $\pm$ 0.017} on ten tasks with 25 successes and 25 failures per task. Atomic tasks were easier than composites. At conformal $\alpha=0.20$, their balanced accuracies were \metric{0.667 $\pm$ 0.030} and \metric{0.590 $\pm$ 0.021}, respectively. Separate atomic-only and composite-only models did not improve matched performance, so mixed-task training was not the main problem.

Semantic Subtask-SAFE fixed the label mismatch for composites and achieved raw held-out segment ROC-AUC \metric{0.734 $\pm$ 0.002}. However, this result did not survive the controls required for an online claim. A causal V2 pilot, a within-horizon target, temporal feature summaries, and 320 additional targeted parents were subsequently tested. The final experiment trained with 280 parents and preserved a frozen 20-parent calibration plus 80-parent evaluation allocation. At causal prefix 32, raw features achieved ROC-AUC \metric{0.512 $\pm$ 0.003}; temporal features achieved \metric{0.566 $\pm$ 0.004}. The parent-bootstrap intervals included chance, and neither detector passed the continuation gates.

The Xiaomi-Robotics-1 extension collected 1,950 new rollouts across 39 official mid-success tasks and pooled them with 692 earlier rollouts. A deterministic 1,950-rollout training export retained 38 trainable tasks after excluding one task with only one failure. The selected MLP and LSTM completed all 810 inner-CV fits and six final refits. Training-only task normalization raised MLP outer-test ROC-AUC from \metric{0.532 $\pm$ 0.016} to \metric{0.692 $\pm$ 0.013}. A 454-rollout independent calibration froze the matched-FPR bundle, which was then applied unchanged to a disjoint 760-rollout balanced prospective shadow cohort. Staged SAFE plus time achieved ROC-AUC \metric{0.9545}, TPR \metric{0.6684}, FPR \metric{0.0500}, and balanced accuracy \metric{0.8092}, versus \metric{0.9522}, \metric{0.6316}, \metric{0.0474}, and \metric{0.7921} for time-only. However, its miss-adjusted alarm was only 0.0259 task horizons earlier, its 95\% bootstrap interval included zero, and recall by the 25\% landmark was only 2.9\%. The preregistered early-warning claim therefore failed.

A follow-up collected 250 fresh Xiaomi parents across five composite tasks with semantic progress recorded online. The parent split was frozen at 170 development and 80 outer-test parents, and terminal-label and subtask-label treatments used identical parent folds. Across 1,620 inner-CV fits and 12 final refits, models were selected on hierarchical task/stage ROC-AUC at the first one and two genuine inferences of the active stage. On the untouched outer parents, the terminal-label MLP achieved \metric{0.653 $\pm$ 0.005}, compared with a stage-conditioned elapsed-time ROC of 0.5. The subtask-label MLP and LSTM achieved only \metric{0.491 $\pm$ 0.004} and \metric{0.494 $\pm$ 0.023}. Thus the current segmented subtask-label training realization did not generalize; only the terminal-label MLP retained early ranking signal.

The subsequent full-parent development run did not fragment semantic stages.
It used only the 170 development parents, allocated as 96 fit, 37 selection,
and 37 calibration parents, while the previously opened 80-parent outer set
remained unscored. A causal MLP with task/stage embeddings, stage-anchor
feature change, progress variables, an active-stage head, and 4/8/16-inference
near-failure heads reached prefix-1/2 task/stage-macro ROC-AUC
\metric{0.696} on selection, versus \metric{0.592} for terminal-label SAFE
and \metric{0.500} for time-only. It beat terminal SAFE on all five tasks and
time-only on four. This is selection-set evidence, not held-out validation.
Only 18 successful calibration parents were available, making a nominal 5\%
strict-crossing conformal threshold maximally conservative and too coarse for
an operating claim.

The later RLDX rollout-level expansion filtered a 35-task natural collection to the 20 tasks with enough support for a fixed calibration/evaluation protocol. It used 1,000 rollouts, an untouched outer split during selection, official inverse-frequency weighting, 810 inner-CV fits, and six final refits. On the balanced 200-rollout evaluation set, the task-normalized LSTM achieved ROC-AUC \metric{0.613}, AP \metric{0.608}, and balanced accuracy \metric{0.578} at the preselected $\alpha=0.15$, compared with \metric{0.500} for the elapsed-time hazard. The gain was concentrated in atomic tasks (0.610); composite performance remained at chance (0.506).

\begin{center}
\fcolorbox{navy}{lightblue}{\begin{minipage}{0.91\textwidth}
\textbf{Decision.} RLDX Subtask-SAFE is useful as a retrospective diagnostic but is not yet a deployable early-warning signal. Xiaomi original SAFE improves prospective classification modestly at matched FPR, but does not provide the required early-warning advantage overall. The five-task follow-up shows that simply cutting parents into independently trained semantic-stage sequences is not a solution. Preserving full-parent causal context and stage conditioning is now the strongest composite-task development direction, but its 0.696 selection ROC must be confirmed on new identities before adding observation/state context or opening a recovery trial.

\subsection{Key findings}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \good\ The $\pi_0$ and RLDX feature-capture paths are official-loader compatible and store one feature per genuine policy inference.
\item \good\ Leakage-free inner cross-validation, fixed held-out evaluation, train-only normalization, and parent-grouped splitting are operational.
\item \good\ RLDX independent MLP features rank failures within tasks; additional data improved stability.
\item \good\ The normal RLDX alpha sweep reproduces the SAFE paper's balanced-accuracy--detection-time evaluation geometry, with misses counted at normalized time 1.
\item \good\ The 20-task natural RLDX expansion found a modest task-normalized LSTM advantage over elapsed time at $\alpha=0.15$, driven by atomic tasks.
\item \bad\ The LSTM was consistently weaker than the independent MLP at these sample sizes.
\item \bad\ Natural prevalence and official inverse-frequency weighting changed results only marginally.
\item \bad\ Separate atomic/composite models did not beat the mixed model.
\item \bad\ Raw Subtask-SAFE ROC was inflated by task/subtask score offsets, unequal priors, and late segment information.
\item \bad\ SAFE did not beat task-conditioned elapsed time in causal-prefix or conformal operation.
\item \bad\ Strong inner-CV results for within-horizon and temporal representations did not transfer to the frozen four-stage evaluation.
\item \good\ Xiaomi original SAFE completed a 39-task collection and a leakage-controlled 810-fit search; task normalization materially improved MLP ranking.
\item \good\ Xiaomi MLP SAFE showed task-macro causal ROC-AUC 0.671 at 10\% and 0.626 at 25\% of task horizon, above the within-task time-only value of 0.5.
\item \bad\ The opened Xiaomi replay did not compare SAFE and time at the same false-positive budget; its promising early alarms therefore remain post-hoc.
\item \good\ Independent Xiaomi calibration matched all detectors at 4.39\% FPR; staged SAFE plus time improved TPR by 0.070 and miss-adjusted detection time by 0.051 task horizons versus time-only.
\item \good\ The frozen prospective Xiaomi test preserved disjoint identities, applied no test-time threshold updates, and achieved staged TPR 0.668 at FPR 0.050 versus time-only TPR 0.632 at FPR 0.047.
\item \bad\ The prospective staged timing gain was only 0.0259 horizons with a bootstrap interval crossing zero; recall by 25\% was 2.9\%, so the preregistered early-warning criteria failed.
\item \good\ In the secondary atomic stratum, SAFE-only reached TPR 0.800 and alarmed 0.300 horizons earlier than time-only; the corresponding composite advantage disappeared.
\item \good\ In the frozen five-task follow-up, terminal-label MLP SAFE achieved active-subtask prefix-1/2 ROC-AUC 0.653 on 80 untouched parents, versus 0.5 for stage-conditioned elapsed time.
\item \bad\ The current segmented subtask-label MLP and LSTM failed to generalize, with outer prefix-1/2 ROC-AUC 0.491 and 0.494 despite stronger inner-CV selection values.
\item \good\ Full-parent stage-aware conditioning improved developmental prefix-1/2 ROC-AUC from 0.592 for terminal SAFE to 0.696 and beat terminal SAFE on all five tasks.
\item \bad\ The stage-aware result has not been tested on new held-out parents; its 18-success calibration set cannot resolve a useful 5\% successful-parent FPR threshold.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope, labels, and experimental rules}

\subsection{Two prediction units}
\textbf{Rollout SAFE} uses the official binary label: success if the RoboCasa terminal task predicate becomes true, otherwise failure at the horizon. \textbf{Subtask-SAFE} predicts whether the current ordered natural-language subtask eventually fails before completion.

One feature record is stored per genuine policy inference. Cached actions from a predicted chunk do not create duplicate feature records. This prevents action-reuse frequency from becoming an accidental sampling weight.

\subsection{Models and selectors}
The rollout-level experiments retained the official SAFE detector families:
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{indep}: an independent MLP detector;
\item \code{lstm}: a temporal LSTM detector.
\end{itemize}
Hyperparameters include horizon selector, diffusion selector, learning rate, and regularization. Unless explicitly noted, the search used three inner folds and model seeds 0, 1, and 2.

Later causal experiments introduce explicitly named models rather than
silently changing official SAFE. Segmented Subtask-SAFE applies the same
official MLP/LSTM families to independent semantic-stage sequences. The
full-parent stage-aware model is a report-specific causal MLP whose optional
components are active-stage and near-failure targets, task/stage embeddings,
causal progress, stage-anchor feature change, and observation/state context.
Non-neural controls are success-prototype distance and task/stage-conditioned
elapsed-time hazard. These families are not interchangeable.

Every result section was audited against the same reporting template:
prediction unit and target; causal inputs and forbidden future information;
parent/task split unit; fit, selection, calibration, and test roles; primary
selection metric; elapsed-time or duration control; uncertainty unit; and
whether the reported identities influenced model selection. Shared official
SAFE settings are defined here, while each $\pi_0$, RLDX, Subtask-SAFE, Xiaomi
broad, prospective, segmented five-task, and full-parent stage-aware section
restates its experiment-specific departures.

\subsection{Evaluation contract}
\begin{itemize}
\item Outer test identities are fixed and never used for hyperparameter selection.
\item Normalization statistics are fitted from training data only.
\item Subtask segments are split by parent rollout before extraction.
\item Raw pooled ROC/AP, task-normalized pooled ROC/AP, and macro task ROC/AP are kept distinct.
\item Duration or elapsed-time baselines are mandatory.
\item A conformal lead time is reported only together with the TPR that determines how often that lead is obtained.
\end{itemize}

\section{\texorpdfstring{$\pi_0$}{pi0} experiments}

\subsection{Training and evaluator smokes}
Independent and LSTM two-epoch evaluator smokes first established that the official SAFE loader, detector checkpoints, and offline evaluator agreed. A two-configuration 1,000-epoch grid pilot then confirmed that the apparently short wall time was expected: with roughly 100 rollouts and batch size 512, one epoch is only a small full-batch update. These runs were engineering gates, not performance claims.

The termination-time duration-only control reached ROC-AUC 0.969 on training
and 1.000 on both seen and unseen validation. Successful episodes often ended
early while failures ran to the horizon, so full-trajectory termination-time
evaluation was duration-confounded. Later causal or matched-earliest results
are reported separately and are the relevant early-warning controls.

An initial six-run all-five-seen fit, before leakage-free hyperparameter selection, produced MLP ROC $0.506\pm0.009$ and PRC $0.541\pm0.019$, versus LSTM ROC $0.702\pm0.031$ and PRC $0.675\pm0.025$. It motivated the full inner-CV experiment but was superseded by the fixed-split result below.

\subsection{Official 810-fit grid}

The LSTM achieved seen \metric{0.792 $\pm$ 0.069} and unseen \metric{0.581 $\pm$ 0.080}. Figure~\ref{fig:pi0grid} visualizes this seen--unseen gap. These are validation results from a small 100-rollout dataset, not a final held-out estimate.

\subsection{Rollout-level conformal calibration}
The 500-rollout dataset was divided into 340 training rollouts, 30 calibration successes, and 130 evaluation rollouts. At $\alpha=0.15$, the MLP achieved TPR 0.196, FPR 0.040, and balanced accuracy 0.578. The originally archived detection-time value 0.716 was conditional on a failure being detected. Under the paper definition, an undetected failed rollout has normalized detection time 1, giving a paper-equivalent average of approximately 0.944. Recall therefore remained too low for reliable recovery.

\subsection{Expanded alpha sweep and paper-equivalent timeliness}
The functional conformal sweep was extended from $\alpha\leq0.20$ to $\alpha\leq0.50$. Increasing $\alpha$ lowers conservativeness, usually increasing both detection coverage and false alarms. Because the official \code{tfunc} modulation itself depends on $\alpha$, the resulting functional bands are not guaranteed to be nested; small non-monotonic changes are therefore possible.

\section{Semantic Subtask-SAFE}

\subsection{Target and decomposition}
The active-stage target is ``does the active natural-language subtask eventually fail before completion?'' A successful parent contributes its completed subtask segments. A failed parent contributes its successful prefix plus exactly one failed active segment; unattempted future stages are excluded.

Predicate-only stages were not sufficient: natural-language tasks can combine several predicates, and adjacent actions may share a predicate. The final schema uses ordered task instructions, merges repeated-predicate stages, drops reset-satisfied stages, and preserves merge provenance.

\section{Subtask-SAFE V2: causal objective and frozen evaluation}

\subsection{Continuation gates and four-stage pilot}
The first causal analysis showed that full-segment discrimination was not a sufficient continuation criterion. V2 therefore introduced four explicit gates: causal-prefix performance, comparison with stage-conditioned elapsed time, parent-bootstrap uncertainty, and an operating point with useful TPR, controlled FPR, and positive lead. The data plan initially selected four stages with both scientific value and some failure support:
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{LoadDishwasher::cup\_on\_rack};
\item \code{PreSoakPan::TurnOnSinkFaucet\_3};
\item \code{ScrubCuttingBoard::cutting\_board\_scrubbed};
\item \code{WashLettuce::water\_on}.
\end{itemize}
Their combined segment deficit was 127. Stage-only conditioning gave mean fold ROC approximately 0.599, while stage plus elapsed progress gave approximately 0.588. A broader policy-only/random-prefix ablation selected horizon \code{concat-2}, diffusion selector 1.0, learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, and regularization 0.01, with inner-validation ROC $0.645\pm0.040$.

The first gated refit did not justify more undirected collection. Its causal-prefix ROC was 0.575 versus 0.673 for elapsed time; TPR was 0.854 only because FPR rose to 0.851. The parent-bootstrap interval for SAFE minus elapsed ROC was $[-0.240,0.037]$. This result motivated a change from the terminal segment label to a within-horizon event target.

\subsection{Within-horizon labels and representation screen}
For a prefix ending at inference $t$, the V2 label asks whether the active stage fails within the next $H$ policy inferences. A feasibility scan showed that $H=128$ with evaluation prefix 32 was the most robust candidate: all three inner folds retained both classes. By contrast, $H=96$ at prefix 48 produced fold ROCs 0.717, 0.250, and 0.400, indicating unstable support.

Fifteen representation/objective treatments were compared on the feasible pilot. Table~\ref{tab:v2-screen} lists the highest-ranking choices. The official SAFE loss on raw features ranked first; temporal summaries were competitive but not uniformly better.

\begin{itemize}
\item \code{LoadDishwasher::dishwasher\_closed};
\item \code{PreSoakPan::TurnOnSinkFaucet\_3};
\item \code{ScrubCuttingBoard::cutting\_board\_scrubbed};
\item \code{WashLettuce::lettuce\_rinsed}.
\end{itemize}
The long collection contributed 120 LoadDishwasher, 120 PreSoakPan, 20 ScrubCuttingBoard, and 30 WashLettuce parents; an additional 30 WashLettuce parents closed the final deficit. Every source dataset passed the rollout, feature, video, and semantic-segment validators.

Before scoring, an immutable target-aware allocation selected five successful calibration parents and 10 successful plus 10 failed evaluation parents per stage. Thus calibration contains 20 parents and evaluation contains 80, with no identity overlap. The remaining 180 eligible new-seed parents were later added to training, while the frozen 100-parent allocation remained byte-for-byte unchanged. The final training manifest contains 280 parents, of which 244 reach a selected target stage; the original old test parents were excluded.

Causal replay asked a deployment-oriented question: if a detector had observed
only the policy inferences available up to the current instant, would it have
raised an alarm on a rollout that eventually failed? This was a \emph{post-hoc
shadow replay}, not a policy intervention: no alarm stopped or changed the
Xiaomi policy. The original 1,824-rollout training split was divided into 1,748
meta-fit rollouts and 76 threshold-validation rollouts. The already opened,
fixed outer test remained 76 rollouts---one success and one failure for each of
38 tasks---and was not used to fit detector parameters or thresholds.

All three detectors were causal: they used the current prefix, current
inference index, known task identity, a horizon estimated from training data,
and whether the rollout was still active. They never used the completed
rollout duration, future observations, Subtask-SAFE labels, or a failure-onset
annotation. The \emph{Model} column in Table~\ref{tab:xr1-causal-online-replay}
identifies the SAFE source architecture:
\begin{description}
\item[Time only] A task-conditioned, monotone estimate of
$P(\mathrm{failure}\mid\mathrm{still\ active\ at\ }t,\mathrm{task})$, fitted on
the meta-fit rollouts. It has no SAFE feature input and is identical for the
MLP and LSTM comparisons.
\item[SAFE only] The running maximum of the current SAFE score, standardized
with task-specific location and scale estimated from the meta-fit rollouts.
It uses no elapsed-time risk as a predictor.
\item[SAFE+time+task] A validation-fitted logistic head combining the current
normalized SAFE score, task-conditioned time risk, normalized causal progress,
their prespecified interactions, and known task identity. The task identifier
is available to the controller; it is not an inferred future label.
\end{description}

Each detector selected its own threshold by validation balanced accuracy, with
ties resolved toward lower FPR and then higher TPR. Therefore the operating
points in Table~\ref{tab:xr1-causal-online-replay} are \emph{not} matched at a
common false-positive budget. ``Event ROC'' ranks rollouts using the maximum
causal score reached anywhere along the full trajectory; it measures
retrospective event discrimination, not how early the alarm occurs. TPR and
FPR are failure recall and successful-rollout false-alarm rate at the selected
threshold, and balanced accuracy is their equal-class summary. ``Detection''
is the mean first-alarm fraction of the task horizon among \emph{detected}
failures only, so lower is earlier but misses disappear from the average.
``Adjusted'' is the stricter timeliness metric: it averages over every failed
rollout and assigns a fraction of 1.0 to a missed failure. Learned-detector
entries are mean $\pm$ population SD over three seeds; the deterministic
time-only row has no seed variation.

\subsection{Frozen matched-FPR calibration and prospective protocol}
The confirmatory experiment is a two-phase shadow protocol. It preserves all
three selected MLP seeds, fits per-seed normalization from the original 1,824
training rollouts, takes each seed's running maximum, and averages the three
normalized trajectories. A new calibration batch targets three successes and
three failures per task (228 primary outcome-quota rollouts, plus retained
overshoot). It
selects global thresholds under one empirical event-level
$\mathrm{FPR}\leq0.05$ constraint. The staged detector permits SAFE from rollout
start through 25\%, suppresses alarms from 25--50\%, and enables the frozen
task-conditioned time fallback from 50\% onward. Its two thresholds are searched
jointly under the same rollout-level FPR cap.

\subsection{Five-task terminal-versus-subtask-label experiment}

The first follow-up tested whether labels aligned to the active semantic
subtask improve early failure ranking on composite tasks. Replay proved slower
than fresh collection and exposed terminal-outcome mismatches, so the final
dataset was collected again with semantic predicates recorded online. It
contains exactly 50 fresh parent rollouts for each of ArrangeBreadBasket,
ArrangeTea, BreadSelection, CuttingToolSelection, and GarnishPancake: 250
parents total. Seventeen stage identities were available for training. Before
model fitting, parent identities were frozen into 170 development and 80 outer
test parents. Terminal-label and subtask-label treatments used the same parent
folds and the outer set remained untouched during hyperparameter selection.

The \emph{terminal} treatment trained original SAFE on each complete parent's
terminal task outcome. The \emph{subtask} treatment exported each observed
semantic stage as a sequence and trained on that stage's eventual outcome;
completed stages were successes, and the active terminally failed stage was a

\subsection{Full-parent stage-aware development experiment}

\subsubsection{Scientific question and data contract}
The segmented result left two explanations confounded: active-stage labels may
be unhelpful, or resetting the representation at every semantic boundary may
discard useful parent-level evidence. The follow-up therefore retained each
complete parent trajectory and constructed one causal row per genuine Xiaomi
policy inference. For an attempted stage, the failure target is zero when the
stage completes and one only for the terminal active stage of a failed parent.
Future unattempted stages are censored and create no rows. The terminal parent
outcome is retained only as an auxiliary or baseline target.

Only the 170 development parents from the five-task collection were loaded.
Task/outcome-stratified allocation occurred before constructing inference
rows: 96 parents for fitting, 37 for model and regularization selection, and
37 for calibration. The previously opened 80-parent outer set remained locked
and unscored. Ten task/stage identities passed the fit-only gate of at least
three successful and two failed stages.

All learned arms used the same causal full-parent SAFE summary: a short-window
trajectory summary and cumulative mean using only features observed through
the current inference. The common neural architecture was a two-layer MLP with
128 hidden units, layer normalization, GELU, dropout 0.1, learning rate
$3\times10^{-4}$, parent/stage/outcome-balanced row weights, and seeds 0, 1,
and 2. Regularization was selected from
$\{10^{-5},10^{-4},10^{-3},10^{-2}\}$. Four neural arms, four regularizers,
and three seeds produced 48 candidate fits and 12 selected refits. No LSTM was
used in this experiment.

\subsubsection{Calibration limitation and evidentiary status}
Threshold calibration treated a successful parent as the conformal unit: any
alarm in any completed stage makes that parent a false positive. Only 18 of
the 37 calibration parents were successful. At target FPR 0.05, the
strict-crossing finite-sample rank is
$\lceil(18+1)(1-0.05)\rceil=19$, exceeding the 18 available successful-parent
scores. The implementation therefore placed every threshold above the maximum
successful score and every detector obtained zero empirical calibration FPR.

At that maximally conservative point, failed-stage TPR was 0.158 for
conditioned, 0.105 for terminal, stage, and prototype, 0.053 for
multihorizon, and 0.526 for time-only. These values do not reverse the early
ranking result: they compare detectors at a coarse boundary forced by
insufficient calibration support. The bounded conclusion is that full-parent
stage-aware conditioning improves \emph{developmental early ranking}. It has
not yet been validated on new parents, and a useful 5\% successful-parent FPR
claim cannot be resolved from 18 calibration successes.

\section{Interpretation across all experiments}

\subsection{What the evidence supports}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The feature-capture and official-export engineering is valid for both
$\pi_0$ and RLDX (Table~\ref{tab:dataset-chronology}).
\item RLDX MLP features contain useful within-task retrospective failure
information (Table~\ref{tab:rldx-scaling}).
\item In RLDX, atomic tasks are materially easier than composite tasks
(Table~\ref{tab:rldx-type-ablation} and
Figure~\ref{fig:rldx-safe20-per-task}); the broad Xiaomi task-type interval is
too wide to generalize this claim (Table~\ref{tab:xr1-task-type}).
\item Task and stage score offsets are large; task-aware normalization is
reasonable when fitted without test leakage and when task identity is
available (Tables~\ref{tab:rldx-scaling} and
\ref{tab:xr1-final-normalization}).
\item Semantic decomposition is necessary for valid subtask labels and grouped
evaluation (Table~\ref{tab:subtask-split} and
Figure~\ref{fig:subtask-support}).
\item Parent-aware causal-prefix testing is necessary before claiming early
warning (Table~\ref{tab:subtask-causal-prefix}).
\item A frozen, stage-balanced evaluation allocation prevents promising but
support-limited pilot results from becoming final claims
(Table~\ref{tab:v2-frozen} and Figure~\ref{fig:v2-frozen-prefix}).
\item Xiaomi's normalized MLP contains causal ranking signal across a much
broader task set, and the frozen prospective protocol validates a modest
classification gain at matched FPR
(Tables~\ref{tab:xr1-matched-fpr-calibration} and
\ref{tab:xr1-prospective-primary}).
\item The prospective secondary stratum identifies a concrete task-type
interaction: SAFE-only is strong and early on atomic tasks, while its timing
advantage disappears on composites
(Table~\ref{tab:xr1-prospective-strata}).
\item On five freshly traced composites, terminal-label MLP SAFE retains
active-subtask ranking at the first one and two genuine inferences
(Table~\ref{tab:xr1-subtask-label-selection}); this is evidence of an early
feature signal, although its mechanism and parent-level uncertainty remain
unresolved.
\item Preserving full-parent causal summaries and adding active-stage,
near-failure, progress, and task/stage conditioning improves selection ROC
from 0.592 to 0.696 and beats terminal SAFE on all five tasks
(Tables~\ref{tab:xr1-stage-aware-selection} and
\ref{tab:xr1-stage-aware-per-task}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What the evidence rejects}
\begin{enumerate}
\item More complex temporal modeling alone: the LSTM was not consistently
competitive (Tables~\ref{tab:rldx-scaling} and
\ref{tab:xr1-final-normalization}).
\item Mixed horizons alone as the cause: split atomic/composite models did not
beat the mixed MLP (Table~\ref{tab:rldx-type-ablation}).
\item Balanced sampling alone as the cause: natural prevalence and paper-style
weighting had negligible paired effects
(Table~\ref{tab:rldx-natural-prevalence}).
\item Semantic decomposition alone as the solution: Subtask-SAFE raw ROC did
not translate into early causal discrimination
(Figure~\ref{fig:subtask-test-auc} versus
Table~\ref{tab:subtask-causal-prefix}).
\item Within-horizon relabeling, stage conditioning, and temporal summaries as
sufficient fixes: all improved selected pilots, but failed to transfer to the
immutable four-stage test (Table~\ref{tab:v2-screen} versus
Table~\ref{tab:v2-frozen}).
\item More of the same target-aware data as a guaranteed remedy: expanding to
280 training parents did not make prefix-32 ROC statistically distinguishable
from chance (Figure~\ref{fig:v2-frozen-prefix}).
\item Raw pooled Xiaomi scores as the deployment variable: training-only task
normalization is necessary for the MLP
(Table~\ref{tab:xr1-final-normalization}), and elapsed time remains a stronger
raw retrospective predictor (Table~\ref{tab:xr1-task-type}).
\item Current Xiaomi terminal-label SAFE as an overall early recovery trigger:
the prospective staged detector missed the 0.10 timing margin, early-landmark
recall target, and bootstrap gate (Table~\ref{tab:xr1-prospective-primary}).
\item Independently segmented active-subtask labels as a sufficient fix: both
subtask-label architectures fell to chance on the frozen 80-parent outer set
despite above-chance inner selection
(Table~\ref{tab:xr1-subtask-label-selection}).
\item The current stage-aware result as a deployment operating point: only 18
successful calibration parents were available, so the nominal 5\% conformal
rank exceeded the available sample and forced zero empirical FPR for every
detector.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why normalization is reasonable, but insufficient}
Training-only task or task/subtask normalization is scientifically reasonable
because different tasks have different score scales and the deployed
controller knows its commanded task. It is analogous to task-conditional
calibration, not test-set tuning. However, normalization can only remove
offsets; it cannot create within-stage ordering. The Subtask-SAFE drop from raw
ROC 0.734 in Figure~\ref{fig:subtask-test-auc} to parent-aware 0.558 in
Figure~\ref{fig:subtask-parent-bootstrap} exposes that limitation. Xiaomi is
the complementary case: Table~\ref{tab:xr1-final-normalization} shows MLP
task-z ROC rising from 0.532 to 0.692, indicating useful within-task ordering
hidden by task offsets. Every policy family still requires its own
training-derived calibration; thresholds and normalization are not
transferable.

\section{Recommended next experiments}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Confirm the selected stage-aware ranking before further model development.} Collect 50 new identity-disjoint parents for each of the same five composites, retain every rollout, and freeze the current runtime bundle, stage catalog, seed/reset family, and prefix-1/2 metric before inspecting outcomes. Do not refit on this confirmation cohort.
\item \textbf{Use explicit continuation gates.} Require conditioned task/stage-macro ROC at least 0.60, conditioned-minus-terminal at least +0.05, and conditioned wins against terminal and time-only on at least four of five tasks. Report parent-grouped task/stage bootstrap intervals; CuttingToolSelection and ArrangeBreadBasket are mandatory diagnostics.
\item \textbf{Develop observation/state context only after confirmation.} The new collection should record causal Xiaomi observation/state history, but that channel should remain unused for the frozen confirmation. If the current conditioned detector passes, use a separate development allocation to train the context arm, followed by another identity-disjoint test.
\item \textbf{Separate ranking validation from operating-point calibration.} A final 5\% successful-parent FPR claim should use at least 100 successful calibration parents with task coverage. Thirty-nine successes are the mathematical minimum for a nontrivial strict-crossing threshold, but remain statistically unstable.
\item \textbf{Require a causal stage tracker.} Measure stage-estimation error separately, then rerun detector evaluation using predicted rather than privileged stage identity. Oracle-stage results are an upper bound only.
\item \textbf{Confirm atomic SAFE separately.} The atomic subgroup is promising but secondary. Freeze the current SAFE-only operating point and collect a new balanced atomic-only cohort before treating its 0.300-horizon timing advantage as operational evidence.
\item \textbf{Use a new prospective seed family for confirmation.} The current test is opened and may be used for development, never for the final dense-model claim. Only after a new prospective pass should recovery be randomized against shadow operation.
\item \textbf{Continue RLDX diagnosis separately.} Stage-specific heads, survival objectives, earlier feature layers, and complementary proprioceptive/contact signals remain appropriate RLDX follow-ups, but they must not be mixed with Xiaomi original-SAFE evidence.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item $\pi_0$ and RLDX use different policies, feature shapes, tasks, and data volumes; absolute values are not direct architecture comparisons.
\item Only three detector seeds were used; $\pi_0$ held-out sets were particularly small.
\item Natural-rate subsets reflect the collected pool, not an unconstrained deployment distribution.
\item Failure support is sparse for many semantic stages; segment classes are strongly imbalanced.
\item Segments within one parent are dependent; only the parent-aware analyses model this hierarchy explicitly.
\item The original causal-prefix study falls to 25 segments at 32 inferences and zero at 64; V2 fixes prefix-32 balance for four stages but does not test unseen task identities.
\item The frozen 100-parent allocation was inspected in an earlier target-aware experiment before the augmented refit. It is a locked paired follow-up set, not a pristine new confirmatory test.
\item Only four composite stages have balanced V2 evaluation support; per-stage confidence remains limited at 10 successes and 10 failures.
\item RoboCasa predicate boundaries are privileged simulator information; a real robot requires a separate online subtask-progress estimator.
\item Conformal validity depends on exchangeability and can fail under task, policy, layout, or seed drift.
\item The broad Xiaomi outer test has only one success and one failure per task; task-level and atomic/composite uncertainty is therefore wide.
\item Xiaomi causal replay is post-hoc because the 76 outer-test trajectories were already opened by raw and normalized analyses.
\item The Xiaomi matched-FPR shadow test is prospective with respect to its
frozen detector bundle, but it is observational replay on completed
trajectories rather than a detector-triggered recovery intervention.
\item The 228-rollout calibration cohort is outcome-balanced by quota. The 454-rollout all-retained stream documents collection cost and sensitivity, but quota stopping prevents treating its class ratio as deployment prevalence.
\item The 760-rollout prospective primary cohort is also balanced by quota;
the 1,643-rollout retained stream reflects task-specific stopping cost rather
than deployment prevalence.
\item The prospective atomic advantage is a secondary subgroup result and
requires a new confirmatory allocation.
\item The five-task active-subtask outer set contains only 80 parents and eight
evaluation stages. Its three-seed SD does not quantify parent, task, or stage
sampling uncertainty; hierarchical bootstrap results are still pending.
\item The five-task outer set has now been opened. Its terminal-MLP advantage
may guide diagnosis but cannot be used to tune and then confirm another model
on the same identities.
\item The tested subtask treatment split parent trajectories into independent
semantic-stage sequences. It therefore evaluates one implementation of
subtask supervision, not the proposed full-parent dense-label architecture.
\item Dense subtask-outcome labels can be anticipatory: early samples in a
stage that eventually fails may be observationally indistinguishable from
success. A separate change-point target is needed to expose this noise.
\item The full-parent stage-aware comparison uses only 37 selection parents
and ten fit-supported task/stage identities. Its 0.696 ROC-AUC selected
regularization and is not a new held-out or prospective estimate.
\item Stage-aware calibration has only 18 successful-parent units. At target
FPR 0.05, conformal rank 19 is unavailable, so all thresholds lie above the
largest successful score and operating-point comparisons are too coarse for a
deployment claim.
\end{itemize}

\section{Metric-reading checklist}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Is the metric retrospective full-segment or causal-prefix?
\item Is the score raw pooled, train-only normalized, or macro per task/stage?
\item Are dependent segments grouped by parent rollout?
\item Was the outer test excluded from hyperparameter selection and calibration fitting?
\item Is elapsed time evaluated on the same identities?
\item For conformal results, are TPR, FPR, balanced accuracy, and lead time reported together?
\end{enumerate}
